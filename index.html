The Ocean Is Vast. So How Did Pirates Find Anyone?

An interactive exploration of perception, geometry, strategy, and probability.

I How Far Can You See?

The first surprise is how far a lookout can actually see. The ocean feels infinite, but from a crow's nest 100 feet up, the horizon is nearly 12 nautical miles away. And you're not looking for the horizon — you're looking for masts. A tall ship's rigging rises 80+ feet above the waterline. Combine both heights and a single lookout can spot a target over 23 nautical miles away.

From a crow's nest, you're watching 1,660 square nautical miles of ocean. That's larger than Rhode Island.

II The Ribbon

Now set that lookout in motion. A pirate ship sailing at 5 knots covers 120 nautical miles per day. The detection circle sweeps forward, painting a ribbon of surveilled ocean 40 miles wide and 120 miles long — nearly 4,800 square nautical miles every day.

But the real insight is what happens when the lane is narrow. Trade routes aren't spread evenly across the ocean. They compress into straits, passages, and coastal channels.

The Windward Passage is 43 nautical miles wide. A pirate's lookout can see 40 miles across. One ship nearly covers the entire strait.

III The Funnel

Geography doesn't just compress lanes — it creates killzones. The Caribbean narrows, the Strait of Malacca, the Horn of Africa: these natural funnels force merchant traffic through predictable corridors. A pirate who understood this didn't search the ocean. They waited.

V The Arms Race

Sections I–IV prove piracy works. But if it works so well, why didn't it destroy all trade? Because merchants adapted — and pirates adapted back. The result is a repeated asymmetric game with a Nash equilibrium: perfect safety costs more than accepting some losses.

Toggle defenses below and watch the arms race play out. The pirate adapts its strategy in real time. The optimal defense level minimizes total cost — piracy losses plus defense spending plus voyage delays.

Without defense, pirates capture a significant fraction of all traffic. This is the baseline — the price of unprotected trade.

The ocean is vast — but trade is predictable, lookouts see far, and geography is a funnel. Pirates didn't find ships by luck. They found them by math.
